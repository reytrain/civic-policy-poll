# GOVT 2305 report and presentation guide

## Assignment checklist

- Topic connected to government; original student work, not a copied past template.
- Eight demographics on Sheet A; twenty policy questions on Sheet B. Printable sheets are at `/questionnaire`, with a paper page break between them. Confirm the instructor accepts online collection; paper answers are not automatically imported.
- At least twenty real participants. In-memory tests and local test responses are not research participants.
- Initial paragraph: topic, why your team chose it, expected findings and anticipated difficulties.
- Final report: topic explanation, actual method/sample, results, surprises and experiences, supported by charts/tables. A couple of pages or longer as needed.
- Finish the week before final exams. The calendar due date is not known to the app.

## Collecting responses

Use the shared link or researcher dashboard QR. Do not offer political cues about how people should answer. Recruit beyond one friendship group when practical, and record how you recruited, the collection dates, and any unusual experiences without collecting respondent identities. The app accepts adults worldwide but does not ask nationality/residency; do not describe the sample as U.S. residents unless your recruitment independently establishes that restriction. No names or accounts are requested.

Do not submit practice answers to the public survey. Test locally. If collecting on paper, keep the two sheets paired without names; do not also ask those people to submit online. Paper counts and website counts must be reported separately unless a reviewed import method is added later.

## Suggested report structure

Topic and motivation; research questions/expectations; methods and voluntary sampling; questionnaire and recruitment dates; actual sample size; overview of all twenty questions; selected demographic comparisons; surprises and collection experiences; limits/privacy; conclusion; chart/table appendix and separate questionnaire sheets.

## Presentation talking points

These are possible connections to American Government concepts, not a claim about your instructor's exact syllabus. Replace bracketed details with observed data.

- **Federalism:** Q4 tax breaks and Q19 utilities connect state/local choices with federal economic policy. Compare residence environments using actual n values.
- **Congress and public finance:** Q1 bailout equity, Q6 farm support and Q12 agency funding ask how taxpayer money is allocated and overseen. Discuss what respondents support, not what these policies necessarily achieve.
- **Interest groups and accountability:** Q10 post-agency employment and Q11 contractor PAC contributions connect government decision-making to organized private interests. Explain any observed differences by political outlook without assuming motives.
- **Economic regulation:** Q7 market divestiture, Q8 marketplace search and Q17 non-competes connect competition to government's role in the economy.
- **Public goods and procurement:** Q15 audits, Q16 repair information and Q20 tolls connect contracts and public infrastructure to oversight and taxpayer interests.
- **Public opinion and research limits:** Compare overall results with a selected cross-tab. Say, "In our voluntary sample, [count] of [group n] chose [answer]." Do not claim a national trend, causal explanation or margin of error from this convenience sample.

Small groups (especially fewer than five) should not be separately presented in public. Keep raw demographic exports private. Store exports safely and agree with the instructor when the Site and collected data should be removed after the project.
